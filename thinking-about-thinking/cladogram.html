Where the colours scatter

A cladogram assumes descent with modification: shared characters mean common ancestry. Philosophy violates that constantly. So branch on real diagnostic characters, colour the tips by actual lineage, and read the mismatch — that is what a cladogram is for.

    The positions

    What the tree shows

    Characters 6 and 7 split the lower half of the tree, and both clades are thoroughly mixed. Yogacara and Husserl are not related; they arrive at pre-reflective self-awareness independently, thirteen centuries apart. Nyaya and David Rosenthal both hold that a mental state is unconscious until a second state targets it, and Nyaya got there in Sanskrit around 200 CE. Madhyamaka’s objection to self-awareness — a blade cannot cut itself — is structurally the objection Ryle would make against the inner eye.

    That scatter is the finding. Position in this tree predicts almost nothing about ancestry. In biology this pattern is called homoplasy, and it is normally a nuisance to be explained away. Here it is the result: the space of defensible positions about consciousness is small enough that traditions with no contact keep landing in the same places.

    The character matrix

    Truth looks like a separate topic but on one major axis it is not. The Indian debate over pramanya asks whether a cognition certifies its own truth (svatah, held by Mimamsa and Advaita) or whether validity must be established by something external (paratah, held by Nyaya). That is character 6 against character 7, applied to a different question — and the same schools land on the same sides.

      Limits worth taking seriously

      The character set is a choice, not a discovery. Biology has a ground truth the tree is trying to recover; here the tree is only as good as the questions you branch on. Pick different characters — intentionality, the mind-body relation, whether error about one’s own states is possible — and you get a different topology with different clades. A cladogram of ideas cannot be wrong in the way a phylogeny can.

      The tips are not comparable units. Nyaya is a thousand-year tradition with fierce internal disagreement, compressed here into one dot beside Descartes, one man. The contested cells hide real fights: Prabhakara and Bhatta Mimamsa split over exactly the self-luminosity question, and Husserl and Sartre disagree about whether there is an ego in consciousness at all.

      Trees cannot show reticulation. Schopenhauer read the Upanishads; Husserl was read by Buddhist studies scholars who then reinterpreted Yogacara through him; Kriegel and Zahavi cite Dharmakirti directly. Some of what looks like convergence in the lower half of the tree may be borrowing running backward through the diagram. A phylogenetic network — a tree with horizontal edges drawn in — would be the honest form.
