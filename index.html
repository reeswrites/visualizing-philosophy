Visualizing philosophy

Diagrams in philosophy aren't an outlier; they're an old tradition that got interrupted. Plato's divided line, Llull's wheels, Euler's circles, Peirce's existential graphs. There is room for more, because structurally complex debates are where prose strains hardest. Each piece below is a separate, self-contained visualization.

  • The free will map

    An interactive map of the free will debate as an aporetic cluster.

  • Belief Mart

    A shop where you assemble a religion from its parts, then find out what you built.

  • Thinking about thought

    Two views on the early history of reflection about the mind: a comparative timeline and a character cladogram.

    Also: ./thinking-about-thinking/cladogram.html

  • Argument mapper

    A dialogue-first argument mapper: the graph is the output of reasoning, not the working surface.

    Runs locally — it needs a server. See arg-mapper/ in the repository.
